Welcome to the **Advanced Water Treatment Plant (AWTP) Lab** at the Department of Environmental Engineering, College of Science and Technology, Korea University. I am **Youngjin Kim**, an Associate Professor directing this research group.

> **"Bridging the gap between fundamental membrane science and practical environmental engineering."** <br>
> Our laboratory is dedicated to addressing global water scarcity and environmental challenges through the development of innovative, sustainable, and energy-efficient water treatment technologies. We aim to design next-generation solutions for water purification, wastewater reclamation, and resource recovery.

<br>

## 🔬 Core Research Areas

We actively conduct interdisciplinary research supported by major national funding agencies and industry partners, including the **National Research Foundation of Korea (NRF)**, **K-water**, and **SK Hynix**. Our primary research themes include:

* 🌊 **Advanced Membrane Processes & Desalination:** Overcoming the limitations of conventional desalination. We evaluate FO/RO hybrid systems for low-energy seawater desalination and fabricate diatom-based membranes for rainwater reuse.
* ♻️ **Resource Recovery & Circular Economy:** Moving beyond simple treatment to recover valuable resources from waste streams. Key projects include ammonia recovery from anaerobic digestion leachate using membrane contactors and economical resource recovery for dissolved ions from desalination concentrate.
* ⚡ **Advanced Oxidation Processes (AOP):** Evaluating and developing AOPs specifically targeted for treating complex industrial effluents, such as semi-conductor and TMAH wastewater.
* 🛡️ **Emerging Contaminants & Novel Hybrid Systems:** Tackling micropollutants through novel Granular Activated Carbon (GAC) & Gravity-Driven Membrane (GDM) hybrid systems, and layered nanomembrane processes for ultrapure water production.
* 🌱 **Sustainable & Low-Energy Technologies:** Developing innovative approaches like the low-energy fertilizer-driven FO integrated system for simultaneous CO2 capture and alkaline wastewater reuse.
