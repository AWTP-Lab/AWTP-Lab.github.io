Welcome to the **Advanced Water Treatment Plant (AWTP) Lab** at the Department of Environmental Engineering, College of Science and Technology, Korea University. I am Youngjin Kim, an Associate Professor directing this research group. 

Our laboratory is dedicated to addressing global water scarcity and environmental challenges through the development of innovative, sustainable, and energy-efficient water treatment technologies. By bridging the gap between fundamental membrane science and practical environmental engineering, we aim to design next-generation solutions for water purification, wastewater reclamation, and resource recovery.

### 🔬 Core Research Areas

We actively conduct interdisciplinary research supported by major national funding agencies and industry partners, including the National Research Foundation of Korea, K-water, and SK Hynix. Our primary research themes include:

* **Advanced Membrane Processes & Desalination:** We focus on overcoming the limitations of conventional desalination. Our research includes the evaluation of forward osmosis (FO) and reverse osmosis (RO) hybrid systems for low-energy seawater desalination, as well as the fabrication of diatom-based membranes for rainwater reuse.
* **Resource Recovery & Circular Economy:** Moving beyond simple treatment, we aim to recover valuable resources from waste streams. Key projects include the evaluation of membrane contactors for recovering ammonia from anaerobic digestion leachate and developing economical resource recovery technologies for dissolved ions from desalination concentrate.
* **Advanced Oxidation Processes (AOP):** We are actively evaluating and developing advanced oxidation processes specifically targeted for treating complex industrial effluents, such as semi-conductor and TMAH wastewater.
* **Emerging Contaminants & Novel Hybrid Systems:** To tackle micropollutants, we develop novel granular activated carbon (GAC) and gravity-driven membrane (GDM) hybrid systems, and layered nanomembrane processes for the control of trace contaminants in ultrapure water production.
* **Sustainable & Low-Energy Technologies:** Our innovative approaches include the development of a low energy fertilizer-driven forward osmosis integrated system for simultaneous CO2 capture and alkaline wastewater reuse.
